About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Artificial Intelligence and Data Science, University of Mysore, and a Ph.D. research scholar. My research applies deep learning to medical imaging and agriculture, with a focus on explainable models and learning from small datasets. I also teach Design and Analysis of Algorithms.

Assistant Professor & Deep Learning Researcher

I build deep learning systems that clinicians and farmers can inspect and trust, and I teach students to reason about algorithms step by step.

My doctoral work sits at the meeting point of the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) and the Internet of Agricultural Things (IoAT): machine learning methods that serve both healthcare and agriculture. Before joining the faculty, I worked as a Project Associate at CSIR-CFTRI, Mysuru, where I built machine learning pipelines for near-infrared (NIR) spectroscopy data, and as a Software Test Engineer at Accenture, where I learned how to make software dependable. Both experiences shape how I supervise projects and how I teach students to test their work.

Current research projects

Explainable segmentation of white blood cells (Eff-UNet3+)

In progress

An EfficientNet-based UNet3+ model that segments white blood cells and uses explainability methods to show which image regions drive each prediction.

Code on GitHub

Few-shot blood cell classification

In progress

Prototypical Networks that classify blood cell types from only a handful of labelled images, including a 13-class white blood cell setup with a MobileNetV4 backbone for lightweight deployment.

Code on GitHub

Few-shot plant leaf disease classification

In progress

A Prototypical Network with a Swin-Tiny backbone that recognises leaf diseases (including paddy) when only a few examples of each disease are available.

Code on GitHub

Mulberry leaf image dataset for silkworm feed

A structured image collection pipeline for mulberry leaves from the university's silkworm-feed plantation, covering lab, greenhouse, and field conditions, to support future leaf quality and disease models.

Teaching

I teach algorithms through real-life analogies, step-by-step traced examples, and pseudocode before code.

Design and Analysis of Algorithms (DAA)

Undergraduate

Topics: asymptotic analysis, divide and conquer, greedy methods, dynamic programming, backtracking, and branch and bound.

Teaching approach

Students learn an algorithm best when they can see it run. Each topic starts with an everyday analogy, moves to a hand-traced example on a small input, then to pseudocode, and only then to code and complexity analysis.
